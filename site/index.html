About

Can a model decide which businesses to onboard? Every KYB-Bench case is a real UK company applying for an account, frozen at a date: the application, its Companies House record as it stood then, archived web evidence, and sanctions and PEP screening. The system decides approve, review or decline under a published policy, and gives a risk score.

Decision models, LLMs and LLM agents are scored on the same hidden test cases: how well the risk score ranks the businesses that should be stopped, the decisions themselves, and what each case costs to run.

  • 2,164 test cases, including 333 with outcomes from after 3 August 2026, past every listed model's training cutoff.
  • Labels from real outcomes (companies later wound up in the public interest), planted issues, and the policy's industry and licence rules.
  • Test labels stay hidden; submissions are scored by our server.

The task

One kind of case, one output, one grader, so the only thing that changes between rows is the system. Each case is a UK business applying to open an account, frozen at a date. The system sees:

Application
Claimed name, number, addresses, directors, owners, website, industry and expected volume.
Registry
The Companies House record as it stood on the case date: status, officers, people with significant control and filings, rebuilt from filing history.
Web
Google results and website text, taken only from archived captures made on or before the case date.
Screening
UK and US sanctions, politically exposed persons, disqualified directors, and the FCA, Gambling Commission and HMRC registers.
Policy
Industry risk tiers and licence rules, the same for every system.

It returns a decision, a risk score and the probability of each issue in a fixed catalogue. Systems run in one of three modes: prompt (one call), workflow (fixed typed questions, with fixed policy code turning the answers into a decision) or agent (any harness, in a locked-down container).

// a prediction
{"case_id": "tf-0042", "decision": "review", "score": 0.71, "industry": "H_INVESTMENT",
 "issues": {"OWNER_MISMATCH": 0.92, "SANCTIONS_HIT": 0.02, ...}}

What the cases contain

Real shutdowns

Companies the Insolvency Service later wound up in the public interest, frozen 90 days before the first public sign.

Planted issues

Ordinary companies with a problem planted in the application, such as an owner missing from the register or a sanctioned director.

Policy cases

High-risk and prohibited industries, missing licences and politically exposed people.

Future risk

Companies whose registered address the registrar later rejected. Scored as a ranking only.

Grading

  1. Ranking: PR-AUC of the risk score, with the cases the answer key declines (real shutdowns, planted issues that warrant a decline, prohibited or unlicensed activity) as positives. It needs no cost assumptions and no thresholds.
  2. Decisions: the share of bad cases declined and the share of good businesses stopped.
  3. Efficiency: every case records a trace, priced from a published monthly price table, so model cost per case is comparable across systems.
  4. Test submissions return aggregate metrics only; groups under 10 cases are not reported.
Approve
A good business with nothing that needs a closer look.
Review
Something an analyst should check: a high-risk industry, a politically exposed person, a licence that may be needed.
Decline
A business the policy says to stop: a real shutdown, a planted mismatch or screening hit, a prohibited or unlicensed activity.

Submit

Any system that maps a case to a decision can enter: a decision model, a classical model, an LLM with a prompt, or an agent.

pip install kybbench
kybbench download                # pilot and validation with labels, test splits without

# run your system: predict(case) -> prediction
kybbench run --cases data/validation/cases.jsonl --system my_system:predict \
  --name my-system --type llm --mode prompt --out runs/my-system
kybbench eval --labels data/validation/labels.json --preds runs/my-system/predictions.jsonl

KYB_SUBMIT_KEY=... kybbench submit --split test_fresh \
  --preds runs/my-system/predictions.jsonl --name my-system
  1. Implement predict(case), recording model calls with the harness so the trace is complete.
  2. Tune on validation only. It is the only labelled data any system may use.
  3. Request a submitter key by opening an issue on GitHub.
  4. Submit test predictions; open a pull request with predictions, traces and metadata to appear here.

Limitations

  • Labels were made by research agents and checked against registers, not audited by people. Industry labels with low confidence are left unscored.
  • Web evidence is thin: few small UK companies have archived websites from before their case date.
  • Real shutdowns are few (59 of 2,164 test cases), so results on them alone are noisy.
  • Finic builds Grayson, one of the evaluated systems. Every system is scored by the same grader on the same hidden labels.
Cases are built from public records of real UK companies (Companies House data under the Open Government Licence). Every application is constructed for the benchmark, some contain deliberately altered details, and nothing here implies wrongdoing by any named company or person beyond existing public records.
